OAT · Conformal Agent Error Attribution

How conformal prediction enters two failure-attribution methods

Both methods have the same shape. Stage 1 gives every step a heuristic score. Stage 2 is split conformal prediction in three steps: ① compute a calibration score on held-out data, ② take a quantile of those scores as the threshold, ③ threshold a new run. The two papers differ in what a calibration score is computed on, and so in what the guarantee promises. OAT bounds how often a normal step is flagged. Feng et al. bound how often the decisive error is missed. All data below are simulated; move α to see each guarantee at work.

Yeh et al. · OAT

OAT: conformal outlier detection on steps

Learns from successful runs only. Calibration needs no labels, and the guarantee is about false alarms.

Stage 1

Scoring: how surprising is each step?

  • Each step becomes a vector \(h_t\) (an LLM's hidden states).
  • A sequence model trained only on successful runs predicts what the step should look like, \(\hat h_t\).
  • The anomaly score is the prediction error \(e_t=\|h_t-\hat h_t\|_2^2\). High means the step leaves the usual path of a successful run.
Stage 2

Conformal prediction: a threshold with a known false-alarm rate

1Calibration score of one step

Run the Stage 1 model on held-out successful runs. Every step of every run is one calibration point, and its score is just its anomaly score. No label is needed, because every step of a successful run is treated as normal.

\[ s\big(\text{step } t \text{ of run } \tau\big) \;=\; e^{(\tau)}_t \;=\; \big\|h^{(\tau)}_t-\hat h^{(\tau)}_t\big\|_2^2 \]

First four held-out successful runs. Each cell is one step and shows its score \(e_t\). Cells above the threshold of step 2 are outlined.

2Threshold = quantile of the pooled scores

Pool all \(N\) step scores and take the \(1-\alpha\) quantile.

\[ \delta=\mathrm{Quantile}\Big(1-\alpha;\ \big\{e^{(\tau)}_t\big\}_{\tau\in D_{\rm cal},\,t\le T_\tau}\Big) \]

The paper uses the plain \(1-\alpha\) quantile; with hundreds of pooled steps the \(\lceil (N+1)(1-\alpha)\rceil\) correction used here changes little.

Histogram of all pooled calibration scores. The shaded tail to the right of \(\delta\) holds a fraction \(\approx\alpha\) of normal steps.

3Flag steps of a new failed run

Score every step of the failed run with Stage 1 and flag those above \(\delta\).

\[ \hat y(x)=\{\,t:\ e_t>\delta\,\} \]

Feng et al. · Conformal Agent Error Attribution

Feng et al.: a contiguous block that covers the decisive error

Calibrates on failed runs with the decisive step labelled. The guarantee is coverage of that step, as in split conformal prediction.

Stage 1

Scoring: a step score from an LLM judge, then a block score

The judge looks at one step \(c_i\), shown together with the conversation it comes from, and returns \(p_i=\mathrm{LLM}(c_i)\in[0,1]\), its belief that the decisive error is in that step. The paper builds this judge in three ways. (AUROCs below are from the paper's Table 2, measured on MATH + GSM8k test runs, not on Who&When.)

From step scores to block scores

Filtration needs a score for a whole block \(c_{j:k}=(c_j,\dots,c_k)\). The main choice sums the step scores and divides by the trajectory length \(\ell\):

\[ g(c_{j:k})=\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{i=j}^{k} p_i \]

A bigger block never scores lower (monotone), which gives the closed-form calibration scores in Stage 2. Max and log-sum-exp aggregations are alternatives. Vanilla CP skips this and uses \(p_i\) directly.

The paper also fixes the ends: \(g(\emptyset)=0\) and \(g(x)=\infty\) for the whole trajectory, so left filtration gives \(S=\infty\) when the error is at \(c_1\) (right filtration when it is at \(c_\ell\)). This page keeps the finite normalized sum for the whole run. Coverage holds either way; only the numbers change.

2Threshold = conformal quantile of the run scores

Each calibration run contributes one score. Sort the \(n\) scores and take number \(k\).

\[ \hat q = S_{(k)},\qquad k=\big\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha)\big\rceil \]

One dot per calibration run (\(n=92\), about half of Who&When's 184; the paper drops a few runs whose labelled error index exceeds the trajectory length). Runs left of \(\hat q\) would have kept their error after filtering.
