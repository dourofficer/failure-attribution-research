\paragraph{Description} Who\&When is the benchmark that defined the task (\S\ref{sec:intro}): every record is the output log of a multi-agent run that \emph{failed} a GAIA or AssistantBench query, plus three labels---\code{mistake\_agent} (who), \code{mistake\_step} (when: a 0-based index into \code{history}) and a free-text \code{mistake\_reason}. Failures are natural (no injection). Two subsets come from two very different MAS:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Algorithm-Generated} (126 logs): teams assembled automatically per task by \emph{CaptainAgent} (AG2, GPT-4o)---a handful of \code{*\_Expert} agents plus a \code{Computer\_terminal}. 82 distinct expert names appear as blamed agents.
\item \textbf{Hand-Crafted} (58 logs): \emph{Magentic-One} (Orchestrator, WebSurfer, FileSurfer, Assistant, ComputerTerminal) on 28 AssistantBench and 30 GAIA tasks.
\end{itemize}
In total 184 logs (reported as 127 MAS configurations in the paper; 124 distinct \code{system\_prompt} dicts in the AG JSONs plus 1 HC). \textbf{Annotation}: three experts, three rounds (independent labelling with uncertainty flags on 15--30\% of cases, consensus discussion, cross-validation); 23--31 h per annotator; no agreement coefficient reported. The decisive step is defined as the earliest step whose correction would have made the run succeed---a single-root-cause assumption later questioned by MP-Bench.

\paragraph{Metrics and baselines} Agent-level and step-level exact-match accuracy, with or without the ground-truth answer given to the attributor. Three prompting strategies were compared (GPT-4o, with ground truth; agent\,/\,step accuracy in \%):

Reasoning models do not help much on Hand-Crafted All-at-Once (averaged with and without ground truth, Table~4: o1 41.4\,/\,10.3, DeepSeek-R1 56.9\,/\,3.5 vs.\ GPT-4o 54.3\,/\,4.4). Step accuracy collapses on the long Hand-Crafted logs.

\paragraph{Schema} One JSON object per log: \code{question}, \code{question\_ID}, \code{ground\_truth} (\code{groundtruth} in HC), \code{is\_correct} (\code{is\_corrected} in HC), \code{level} (GAIA 1--3 as int in HC or string in AG; AssistantBench Medium--Hard in AG only, absent in HC AssistantBench), \code{history} (list of \{\code{role}, \code{content}\} in HC and \{\code{role}, \code{name}, \code{content}\} in AG), \code{system\_prompt} (AG only), and the three labels. In Hand-Crafted logs \code{history[0]} is the human question, and orchestrator thoughts, orchestrator instructions and specialist replies are separate steps; in Algorithm-Generated logs the first entry is already an expert message.

\paragraph{Notes} (i) Two regimes in one benchmark: short, synthetic-team logs (Alg.-Gen.) versus long Magentic-One logs (Hand-Crafted, 34 of 58 have $\ge$30 steps). (ii) Partial observability: logs store message texts (plus AG \code{system\_prompt} dicts, orchestrator thoughts/ledgers, OCR/metadata, and terminal outputs), but no raw screenshots (only \code{<Image>} placeholders), structured tool/API traces, environment state, or replay code (the setting TraceElephant criticises). (iii) Data and label quirks: inconsistent agent-name casing (\code{WebSurfer}/\code{Websurfer}); \code{mistake\_step} is a JSON string in both subsets (while \code{level} is an int in GAIA HC vs.\ a string in AG); subset-specific keys (\code{groundtruth}/\code{is\_corrected} in HC vs.\ \code{ground\_truth}/\code{is\_correct} in AG), plus sparse extra fields (4 HC logs carry \code{mistake\_type}; AG \code{30.json} adds \code{labels}); 5 JSON logs (2 HC, 3 AG) have \code{mistake\_agent} differing from the speaker at \code{history[mistake\_step]}; and the Parquet files drop \code{level} (both) and \code{system\_prompt} (AG), with \code{Hand-Crafted.parquet} containing 3 out-of-bounds \code{mistake\_step} values (\code{34}, \code{53}, \code{54}) that disagree with the JSON logs (prefer the JSON files). (iv) Overlaps: the same logs are re-annotated by three experts in MP-Bench (177 of its 289 items); TraceElephant re-runs the same two MAS with full traces; Who\&When~Pro is the scaled-up, injected-failure successor.
